# geez·art — share copy for the Ethiopian & diaspora community

Who this is for: **Ethiopian and diaspora creators on TikTok, Instagram
Reels, Telegram, and Facebook** — the people who will actually *make* and
*share* mosaics, not just read about the app.

Tone: **warm, proud, plain verbs, no hype.** The script is sacred to the
Orthodox tradition, so everything is framed as a **tribute** — never a meme,
never "funny," never a gimmick.

**The golden rule (repeat it in every platform's pinned post):**
If a mosaic shows a recognizable person, ask that person before sharing it.
A portrait in fidel is still a portrait of someone real.

Why this works with this audience (the facts behind the copy):

- Amharic-content culture is strong on TikTok (`#ethiopian_tiktok`,
  `#amharatiktok`) and Instagram — Amharic hooks outperform English ones.
- People already pay for fidel learning apps ($0.99–4.99) — a free tool that
  *uses* the letters for art has an easy yes.
- Bold, high-contrast subjects make the best mosaics — say so plainly, and
  tell people exactly what to try.
- The one-line promise of the app: **a picture at a distance, letters up
  close** — that's the hook that carries almost every post below.

## What to film (the satisfying transform)

The core clip every platform can run: **source photo → letters assemble →
zoom in to see they're real glyphs → zoom out to see the face.**

1. **The transform.** Hold the source photo, cut to a screen recording, and
   slide the width slider up from 20 → 200+ columns so the mosaic visibly
   assembles live.
2. **The double reading in one shot.** Zoom in until it's obviously letters,
   zoom out until the face appears. That IS the app's whole promise.
3. **Palette flip.** Cycle Mono → Parchment → Icon → Church mural on the same
   photo — four moods in ten seconds.
4. **Dithering flip.** Ordered → blue noise → Floyd–Steinberg side by side, so
   people see the modes aren't marketing.
5. **Tap the demo buttons.** Icon, Face, Jebena, Cross, Checker — zero effort,
   instant content.
6. **The export moment.** Hit Download PNG and show the file landing in the
   camera roll — makes it feel real and usable.
7. **The framed result.** Print one and put it in a frame on a wall; film the
   finished object, not just the screen.
8. **Reaction format.** Your face → the mosaic → your face again. Simple and
   shareable.
9. **"Guess the photo."** Show the mosaic first, reveal the source at the end.
   Strong engagement bait, cheap to make.
10. **The number overlay.** "120 × 160 = 19,200 letters" as a caption line or
    on-screen text — the scale is part of the awe.

## 3 demo-image subjects that will read best as mosaics

These are the three to put in your first posts — they reliably come out
looking like the subject, not a texture:

1. **A profile portrait with one hard light.** A face against a plain
   background with strong side lighting. Faces carry the most information, and
   the app's edge emphasis keeps the jaw, brow, and nose crisp while skin
   tones dither cleanly. Highest wow factor, and every viewer has one.
2. **A jebena (Ethiopian coffee pot) on parchment.** A bold silhouette on a
   light, flat background. High contrast, simple shape, instantly
   recognizable, deeply cultural. It reads beautifully even at low column
   counts, so it's the safest first post.
3. **An Ethiopian cross — or a church silhouette — against a bright sky.**
   Geometric, symmetric, high contrast, and it carries the respectful framing
   on its own. Shows beautifully in the Icon or Church mural palette.

Bonus: a **Mesob woven-basket pattern** or a **coffee-ceremony still** — the
repeating texture holds up across many column counts, so it demonstrates the
width slider like nothing else.

## Posting notes

- **Lead with the Amharic line, follow with English.** A short Amharic hook
  (ከሩቅ ሥዕል፣ ከቅርብ ፊደል) rides the existing `#amharatiktok` wave; the English
  follow-up opens it to the wider diaspora.
- **Post evenings Addis Ababa time** for the home audience, weekends for the
  diaspora — the overlap is the sweet spot.
- **First comment carries the hashtags** on Instagram; keep the caption
  itself short and human.
- **Never meme the script.** No "this is what your ex looks like as a font."
  The line is tribute, always.
- **Reuse the footer rule in your pinned post:** *if a picture includes
  someone, ask before sharing it.*
