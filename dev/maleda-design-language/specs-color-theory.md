Generated by the 15-agent color-harmony fan-out. All hue angles are CIELAB hue_ab.


## Manuscript palette harmony grammar — the color-wheel theory layer under the Ethiopian Classical color tokens

**Verdict:** The token system is value-correct but harmony-blind. It asserts relationships it never measures, forbids pairs without a general law, never maps the palette onto the wheel, and has no extension or theme-invariance principle — so it cannot say why these hues cohere, what may be added, what must never be, or how harmony survives a theme flip. Four concrete failures: (1) madder↔verdigris is asserted as "complementary," but in CIELAB hue it measures 120.6° — a near-triadic spacing, not a 180° complement. The identity's triad is real, but its third leg is gold's LIGHT (gold enters by luminance/chroma), not its hue; the true equilateral triad is madder/verdigris/indigo. (2) The cited "gold↔ochre ΔE 3.0 deutan collision" is not reproducible under any standard metric (ΔE2000 = 20.5, ΔE76 = 32.8, post-deutan ΔE2000 = 13.6). The real collision mechanism is hue-angle proximity (28.3°) inside the wedge where deuteranopes compress hardest — and the WORST sibling pair in the system is gold↔saffron at 6.1° of hue (ΔE2000 6.8, ΔL 6.7), which the spec defuses only by role-lock, never by law. (3) There is no allowed/forbidden scheme list, no general channel-separation law (only isolated prohibitions), no forbidden hue-zone boundary, and no extension admission test. (4) Nothing states that the harmony GRAMMAR must be invariant across light/dark — the blend mechanic is specified, the invariance principle is not.

Structure in three sentences: the system is a 42° WARM WEDGE (madder 38.7° → gold 80.7°, parchment extending to ~91°), one COOL COUNTERPOINT 78.6° past the wedge (verdigris 159.3°), and one SHADOW point (indigo 289.1°). Everything chroma-carrying in the accent set except verdigris lives inside the wedge. The wheel sector 180°–270° (cyan→blue→violet) is EMPTY — that emptiness IS the system's identity.

════════ 2. THE WHEEL RELATIONSHIPS ACTUALLY PRESENT ════════
A) Madder ↔ verdigris is NOT complementary — it is a 120.6° TRIADIC opposition. The true complement of madder is 218.7° (blue); of verdigris 339.3° (magenta); both are absent. The strongest pair in the system is a near-equilateral spacing, and this is a FEATURE: true 180° complements placed adjacent vibrate via simultaneous contrast (the red/green buzz); a 120° pair is the calm-but-alive distance. The tradition's palette is physically incapable of buzzing — restraint by structure, not willpower.
B) Gold / saffron / ochre / umber ARE analogous: one continuous arc 52.4°→80.7° (28.3° span), plus umber as the arc's desaturated core (chroma 18 vs the pigments' 60–68). Gold is the wedge's brightest high-chroma point; umber is its earth anchor; madder is the wedge's hot, saturated red edge. Parchment (85–91°) is the wedge's pale extension — the ground itself belongs to the same hue family, which is why every pigment on parchment is pre-harmonized.
C) Umber IS the warm neutral: hue 55–65°, chroma 18. It is not hue-neutral — it is the wedge's own earth, so it harmonizes with every warm pigment by hue and with cool pigments by contrast. Indigo IS the cool support: 289.1°, chroma 13, L 10 — a nearly-black blue-violet that functions as shadow, never as a foreground hue.
D) The TRUE triadic spine is madder(38.7°)/verdigris(159.3°)/indigo(289.1°) — spacings 120.6°/129.8°/109.6°, near-equilateral. The OPERABLE data triad is madder/verdigris/gold: gold replaces indigo as the third leg by entering on LIGHTNESS (ΔL 27 from madder, L 65 vs indigo's L 10) — the triad's "gilding." Both statements are true and must coexist in the doc: the hue triad (for depth/shadow) and the light triad (for data/status).
E) The cool support of the split-complementary: madder's true complement lands in the forbidden zone, so the usable "split" is the two warm flanks of the wedge (madder 38.7° + gold 80.7°, 42° apart — analogous) played against the single cool verdigris (78.6° and 120.6° from them). That IS the sanctioned accent scheme: one analogous warm pair + one cool counterpoint.

════════ 3. ALLOWED HARMONY SCHEMES ════════
1. ANALOGOUS (default/secondary — the whole light theme is one): 2–3 members of the warm wedge at different chroma/lightness — madder+gold+umber, saffron+ochre+umber. The 60-30-10 structure IS an analogous scheme: 60% ground (parchment = wedge's pale end), 30% umber (wedge's desat core), 10% one warm accent. Legal only if each adjacent pair differs in ≥2 of {hue ≥25°, ΔL ≥15, ΔC ≥20} (the Second-Channel Law, §6).
2. SPLIT-COMPLEMENTARY (the accent scheme): one warm pair from the wedge + verdigris as the sole cool. This is the identity's "triadic spine" realized as reduced split-complement. It is the ONLY legal warm/cool construction.
3. THE TRIADIC SPINE (signature): madder/verdigris/gold for DATA (positive/negative/live); madder/verdigris/indigo for DEPTH (weave shadows, panel cavity). Never all four of madder+verdigris+gold+indigo in one view.
4. MONOCHROMATIC/VALUE HARMONY (the terminal resting state, the calm view): one pigment at multiple steps (gold 300/500/700, or umber 100→900). This is the terminal's near-monochrome discipline the type spec demands — color = one family, hierarchy = value.

════════ 4. FORBIDDEN SCHEMES ════════
1. FULL TETRAD (two complementary pairs): the palette physically cannot — the complements are absent — and four chroma families violate the max-two law. Forbidden by construction and by law.
2. HIGH-CHROMA COMPLEMENTS ADJACENT: madder 500 adjacent verdigris 500 at equal size/equal lightness. The palette's only near-complement pair; legal only when separated by ≥20 ΔL (madder 700 vs verdi 500, or madder 500 vs sage 300) or by one intermediate neutral. The weave loader's three equal pigments (madder/saffron/verdigris) is the SINGLE sanctioned full-chroma trio — it is the loudest moment in the system and must stay the only one; every other container obeys max-two.
3. THE FLAG: saffron + verdigris + madder co-present in one frame = the Ethiopian tricolor. Also a near-equal-chroma arrangement of three loud hues. Forbidden in any single container (already in the frame spec — here is the theory reason).
4. THE EMPTY ZONE: any hue in 180°–270° at chroma >25 (pure cyan/teal/blue/violet, neon). Indigo is the only legal cool and it is C13. Anything that enters the empty wedge at high chroma breaks the "old palette" character immediately.
5. SAME-WEDGE SIBLINGS WITH <2 SEPARATING CHANNELS: gold↔ochre, gold↔saffron, pale-gold↔onink-saffron as adjacent data channels. (§6.)

════════ 5. WHY THE TRADITION HARMONIZES (the theory) ════════
(a) EARTHY, MID-CHROMA PIGMENTS. Every chroma-carrying hue is mid-chroma (C43–68 max; a modern saturated cyan is C80+). Mid-chroma colors recede into the ground, so three strong accents can coexist without chaos and the 60-30-10 can hold. The manuscript sources (black body, rubrication red, gold gilding, verdigris, saffron, ochre on parchment) are all natural pigments — earths, metal salts, plant lakes — none of which reach the spectrum's extremes.
(b) A WARM NEUTRAL GROUND. Parchment carries hue 85–91° at chroma 3–15 — a yellow-leaning white. Because the ground belongs to the same hue family as the pigments, every pigment on parchment shares a warm component: even verdigris reads as "cool within warm," never foreign. This is the deep reason the palette coheres on a gray ground it would not.
(c) ONE LOUD RED, AND ONLY ONE. Rubrication is a reserve: one saturated red for emphasis only. With a single loud hue, the eye's attention is auctioned to one place — the alert/negative state, the rubricated line. Madder at C61 is the system's most saturated hue, yet it sits at hue 38.7° (red-orange, not pure red 0°), so even the loud red leans toward brown and stays sub-scream.
(d) GOLD AS METAL-LIGHT, NOT A COLOR. Gold (C60, L65, hue 80.7°) reads as illumination because it is the wedge's brightest high-chroma member, it is dosed at ~3%, and it is never a fill. It is manuscript gold-leaf catching light, not a hue to compare against other hues. Capping gold is what keeps it precious; more gold reads as brass.
(e) THE EMPTY WEDGE. The blue-green→violet sector (180°–270°) is deliberately unoccupied. Ancient palettes feel classical precisely because they are built from a restricted arc of the wheel; this palette occupies ~1/3 of it and lets emptiness do the work. Verdigris — a natural copper-acetate patina green at C43 — is the single cool, and even it is muted and warm-leaning.
(f) TRIADIC SPACING PREVENTS BUZZ. The strongest pair sits at 120.6°, not 180°. True complementaries adjacent vibrate; a 120° pair is calm-but-alive. Combined with (b) and (c), the palette achieves restraint by structure.
(g) WARM BOOKENDING. Both neutrals are hue-committed: parchment is yellow-leaning (85–91°), ink is RED-leaning (hue 8.8°, chroma 4.4 — a warm black, not neutral). So BOTH themes are "warm on warm," and this is the mechanism that lets one grammar survive light and dark.

════════ 6. PAIRING RULES — THE SECOND-CHANNEL LAW AND THE ADJACENCY MATRIX ════════
THE LAW: any two colors used as sibling channels (adjacent data series, status, or legend categories) must differ in ≥2 of three perceptual channels: hue (≥25° hue_ab), lightness (≥15 ΔL), chroma (≥20 ΔC). Pairs separated by hue ALONE inside the warm wedge are the forbidden ones — hue-only separation is exactly what deutan compression destroys. (This is the same law the on-ink ramp already applies to states; here it is generalized to every pair in the system.)

MAY SIT TOGETHER (safe pairs, measured):
· Umber (any step) ↔ ANY pigment. Chroma 18 is the lowest of any pigment; it is the harmonizer. ΔE2000 to every accent ≥ 32. Umber↔ochre are hue-adjacent (2.6°) but chroma-separated by 50 — legal by the law.
· Parchment ↔ ANY pigment: near-achromatic ground accepts any figure. ΔE2000 34–57.
· Ink 900/950 ↔ ANY pigment: warm-black accepts any figure.
· Gold ↔ madder: 42.0° hue, ΔE2000 38.6, ΔL 27 — analogous-adjacent, separated strongly by lightness. The LIVE eye-dot + ALERT state never co-occur by law, but as legend categories they are legal.
· Verdigris ↔ gold (the delta/live core pair): 78.6° hue, ΔE2000 37.6. THE single sanctioned warm/cool pair — the skeleton of the data grammar (up = verdi, live = gold).
· Madder ↔ verdigris: ΔE2000 59.0, 120.6° apart, ΔL 12.7. The signature opposition, legal ONLY when separated by ≥20 ΔL or an intermediate neutral (rule 4.2).
· Verdigris ↔ saffron: ΔE2000 43.1, ΔL 21 — legal as legend categories; forbidden only inside a frame with madder (flag rule).
· Indigo ↔ any: C13 nearly-black, a shadow, cannot conflict. But ΔE2000 indigo↔ink900 = 11.2 — indigo is invisible on ink and must never carry text or data there.

NEVER TOUCH (forbidden pairs, with measured justification):
1. GOLD ↔ SAFFRON — the worst pair in the system. 6.1° hue, ΔE2000 6.82 (post-deutan 5.01), ΔL 6.7. Only lightness separates them; any deutan observer + small mark size collapses them to one color. The spec's role-lock (gold = eye-dot/knot/LIVE only, saffron = caution text) is the correct defuse — keep it, and ADD the law: never a gold mark beside a saffron mark.
2. GOLD ↔ OCHRE — 28.3° hue, ΔE2000 20.5, but post-deutan 13.6 (−34%, the largest collapse in the system) and ΔL only 5.3. Two channels separated by hue alone at near-equal lightness, in the wedge where deutan compresses hardest. Forbidden as sibling data channels (the spec's rule stands; its "ΔE 3.0" number should be replaced by this mechanism).
3. PALE-GOLD ↔ ONINK-SAFFRON — ΔE2000 9.18, ΔL 0.4. Practically identical. Forbidden as siblings. On ink, #E5C193 must never sit beside #E8C46A; the spec's "pale gold stays one step toward parchment-tan so it never equals saffron" is the correct guard — make it a law.
4. SAFFRON ↔ OCHRE co-presence — ΔE2000 18.7, ΔL 12.1: separable as channels, mutually exclusive BY ROLE. Both are the warning family; co-presence doubles alarm weight and leans into flag adjacency. One per viewport (already law; theory confirms).
5. SAFFRON + VERDIGRIS + MADDER in one frame = the flag (rule 4.3).
6. MADDER 500 ↔ VERDIGRIS 500 as equal-weight fills (rule 4.2).
7. INDIGO ↔ INK as text/data (ΔE 11.2, invisible); indigo is shadow-only.
(Orthogonal, from the token spec: 500-steps never carry small text on parchment — verdi 500 = 4.1:1, gold 500 = 2.5:1, both fail AA. Contrast law, separate from harmony law.)

The delta pair survives CVD because it obeys the law: onink-red #E8836F ↔ onink-green #7BC9A8 differ by hue AND lightness (ΔE2000 49.8 pre / 26.1 post-deutan, ΔL 9.9) — post-sim ΔE stays 26. If the two were equal-lightness, they would collapse. Never equalize their lightness.

════════ 7. HOW HARMONY SURVIVES ACROSS THEMES (invariance principle) ════════
The GRAMMAR is theme-invariant; only L and C rescale. The light theme is the warm analogous scheme on a warm-yellow ground; the dark theme is the SAME wedge on a red-leaning black, with accents lifted toward parchment (300-tints). Because both grounds are warm (parchment 85–91°, ink 8.8°), the wedge/counterpoint/empty-zone structure is identical in both themes. THE INVARIANCE BUDGET: for every accent token, the hue_ab of its light and dark variants must differ by <5° (verified: gold 80.7°/pale-gold 77.0° = 3.7; madder 38.7°/onink-red 37.1° = 1.6; verdigris 159.3°/sage 162.0° = 2.7; saffron 74.6°/onink-saffron 87.7° = 13.1 — the saffron pair is OUT OF BUDGET and should be corrected toward ~79° if the two are ever to be compared across themes). A theme flip may change L and C only — never a hue crossing into the empty zone, never a theme-dependent hue swap (a green that goes blue in dark mode breaks the wedge). Test: render the same legend in both themes; every pair's hue_ab separation must be within 5° of the other theme's.

════════ 8. EXTENDING WITHOUT BREAKING (the admission filter) ════════
A new hue may enter only if it passes ALL of: (1) it lands in an allowed zone — the warm wedge 38.7°→91°, OR the cool counterpoint 155°–165°, OR the shadow slot 285°–295° at chroma <25; never 180°–270° at chroma >25; (2) it is ≥25° hue from any existing channel hue, or ≥15 ΔL, or ≥20 ΔC from it (Second-Channel Law); (3) its tints/shades blend toward parchment/ink, never white/black (same law as existing scales); (4) it is introduced at one role, one step, at <1% coverage, and only after the existing 5-step scale fails. Safe candidates that pass the filter today: a copper/rust at ~45–50° (sits between madder and ochre, legal only vs both by ΔL/ΔC), a deeper bronze at ~70° C40 L45, a faded rose (madder 100, hue ~42° C30 — already exists), a slate at 250° chroma <20 (shadow only). Forbidden extensions: pure cyan/teal (the verdigris slot is taken), violet/magenta, electric blue, any neon. When in doubt, extend by VALUE (new step of an existing pigment), then by CHROMA (desat variant), and only then by HUE.


## Weight — the theory layer beneath the color tokens (luminance hierarchy / temperature balance / saturation discipline / vibration theory)

**Verdict:** The palette has colors and rules but no physics. The spec tells you WHAT to use and WHERE (values, 60/30/10 areas, per-pigment allowed scopes, WCAG pairs) but never WHY a color carries weight — so every restraint is a remembered commandment rather than a derived consequence. Concretely, five failures of the theory layer: (1) "gold capped ~3%" and "accent ≤10%" are asserted, never derived: there is no relative-luminance table, no luminance-budget argument tying AREA to Y, so nothing explains why madder can inhabit the full 10% but saffron only 6.6% and parchment-gold 3.6% — the cap is not verifiable, and a future editor cannot tell a legal extension from a hierarchy inversion. (2) There is no stated luminance STAIRCASE. The light theme's legibility depends on the pigment band living BETWEEN the bright wall (L* ≥ 86) and the dark panel (L* ≤ 8) — pigment is mid-key on the wall, bright-key on the panel — but the spec never names that ordering, so a new surface or a new pigment has no placement rule. (3) Temperature is described (warm field, two cool accents) but not axiomatized: no a*/b* axis definitions, no "max one foreground cool per viewport" law, and — critically — no trigger grammar for WHEN a cool may enter the field, so verdigris/indigo float as decoration risk. (4) Saturation discipline ("full-chroma pigments never against each other") is stated as fiat with no thresholds: no neutral-seat chroma rule (ground C ≤ 20), no minimum chroma ratio, no "both C ≥ 28 → no shared boundary" number, and the flag law has no perceptual cause given (equal-chroma 120° hue span). (5) Vibration is reduced to a single ΔE 3.0 deutan number on gold/ochre. There is no contrast-zone dial (1.15 / 3.0 / 4.5), no distinction between halation (gold on parchment — the sanctioned gilding float) and complement-shimmer (madder/verdigris — the forbidden red/green), and no separation prescription with thresholds. Net: the system is enforceable only by memorizing rules, not by checking a derived quantity.

# THE THEORY OF WEIGHT — luminance, temperature, saturation, vibration

Synthesis in one sentence: THE PAGE IS A LUMINANCE STAIRCASE (bright wall → mid pigment → dark panel) TRAVERSED BY A WARM TEMPERATURE FIELD WITH EXACTLY TWO COOL BREAKS AND EXACTLY ONE CHROMA FIGURE AT A TIME. Every color's behavior — whether it can carry text, whether it must be a 12px dot, whether it vibrates — is fixed by two measured quantities, its relative luminance Y and its chroma C; the entire discipline is keeping Y-band and C-band usage inside the ratios below. The tokens in specs-deep-dive.md are the output of these laws; this spec is the input.

All Y/L*/C/hue values below are measured (sRGB → relative luminance Y per WCAG, CIELAB L*C*h). Ground values cited against parchment-100 #FCF9F3, parchment-300 #F5E9D1, parchment-500 #E6D6BC (wall / cards / wells) and ink-900 #15090B (panels).

## §1 LUMINANCE HIERARCHY — the 60-30-10 as weight, not just area

### 1.1 The three-band staircase (the load-bearing ordering)
60/30/10 is conventionally AREA. The theory layer restates it as LUMINANCE-ENERGY = Σ(areaᵢ × Yᵢ), and derives the cap law from it. First, the measured bands:

| band | role | tokens | Y range | L* range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GROUND (60%) | walls, cards, wells | parch-100 .949 / -300 .823 / -500 .685 | 0.685–0.949 | 86–98 |
| PIGMENT (10%) | accents | 700-steps for text; 500-cores for marks | 0.037–0.437 | 22–72 |
| PANEL (30%) | the "paintings" + umber structure | ink-900 .0038, ink-950 .0021; umber-500 .096, umber-700 .051 | 0.002–0.096 | 2–37 |

THE ORDERING IS THE LAW: in the light theme, pigment L* sits BETWEEN wall L* and panel L* — the panels are the darkest objects on the page (the wells), the pigments are the mid-key figures, the wall is the bright frame. If a pigment ever sits outside its band (a bright gold on the bright wall as a FILL, a dark madder on the dark panel), the staircase inverts and the page reads as either busy or invisible.

Measured energy split: with wall=parch-300 (Y .823) at 60% area, secondary=umber-500 (Y .096) at 30%, accent=madder-500 (Y .102) at 10%, the luminance-energy split is GROUND ≈ 93% / SECONDARY ≈ 5% / ACCENT ≈ 2%. This skew is WHY the page feels calm: the area-dominant layer is also luminance-dominant. The "calm" is not an aesthetic; it is the energy split. Any revision that moves the secondary layer's Y toward the ground (a brighter panel) collapses the split toward 60/30/10 in energy too and the design stops feeling like a gallery.

### 1.2 The area-cap law (derives the 10% and the 3% gold cap)
Constraint: the 10% accent band must not out-weight the 30% secondary band (or the "paintings" lose dominance):

  area_accent × Y_pig ≤ area_secondary × Y_umber  →  Y_pig ≤ 3 × Y_umber = 3 × 0.0956 = 0.287

Check every pigment core against this ceiling — a pigment is only allowed the nominal 10% if its Y ≤ 0.287; brighter pigments must shrink:

| pigment core | Y | max legal area | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| madder-500 | 0.102 | >100% | full 10% legal |
| verdigris-500 | 0.193 | >100% | full 10% legal |
| ochre #E46F30 | 0.281 | ~10% | exactly at ceiling |
| gold-500 #C9962E | 0.344 | ≤ 8.3% | identity cap 3% |
| gold-300 #E1C488 | 0.573 | ≤ 5.0% | |
| saffron-500 | 0.437 | ≤ 6.6% | |
| parchment-gold #F8E6B8 | 0.800 | ≤ 3.6% | — this IS the ~3% gold cap, derived |

The "gold capped ~3%" in the spec is therefore a CONSEQUENCE: parchment-gold's luminance (Y 0.80, nearly as bright as the wall itself) buys it the tightest area ceiling in the system (3.6%). Gold-500's identity cap (3%) is a restraint ABOVE this legal ceiling (8.3%) — the budget stops inversion, the restraint enforces scarcity for identity. Saffron at 6.6% is the second-tightest, which is why saffron appears only as chips/status, never ambient. Madder and verdigris are the only pigments that can actually inhabit the full 10%, which is why they are the workhorse data valences.

### 1.3 Weight determines role (the sub-staircase inside the pigment band)
On the bright wall, an accent's L* fixes what it may do:
- L* < 45 → text-capable (700-steps: madder-700 L*22.8 / 11.4:1, verdi-700 L*30.0 / 8.9:1, gold-700 L*39.4 / 6.3:1, saffron-700 L*43.5 / 5.4:1, ochre-700 L*36.1 / 7.1:1 — all ≥ 4.5:1).
- 45 < L* < 72 → mark-capable only (500-cores on the wall: madder-500 6.6:1, verdi-500 4.1:1 — clean figure but not AA text; gold-500 2.5:1, saffron-500 2.05:1 — neither text NOR mark; they are wash/glow/ink-panel colors).
- L* > 72 → the near-wall brights (parch-gold 1.18:1, pale-gold 1.6:1) are invisible on parchment; they exist ONLY to be figure on the dark panel.

On the dark panel the ladder inverts: a pigment is figure only as its 300-TINT (L* 65–80), never as its dark core (verdi-500 on ink-900 = 4.5:1, marginal; madder-500 on ink = 2.8:1 — in the vibration zone; gold-500 on ink = 7.3:1 but the design prefers pale-gold at 11.5:1). So the same hue is re-weighted per ground — see §6.

### 1.4 Dark theme: the staircase runs the other way
Dark: canvas ink-900 Y .0038, panels sink to ink-950 Y .0021, raised cards umber-900 Y .0091. The surfaces are all near-black (L* 2–8); WEIGHT IS CARRIED BY THE TEXT, not the surfaces. The parchment text ramp is the bright layer: parch-100 Y .949 (18.6:1), parch-600 .542 (11.0), parch-900 .221 (5.0). This is the same staircase read from the bottom: the panels are the wells, the text is the figure, and the pigments ride the text's weight band.

## §2 TEMPERATURE BALANCE — the warm field and its two cool breaks

### 2.1 The measured temperature map (CIELAB a*, b*)
The palette is a WARM WORLD: 6 of 7 pigment families plus both neutrals are warm, and even the "black" is warm-leaning.
- Warm field: parchment b* +3.2 to +15 (yellow pull); umber a* +7.8..+11, b* +16; gold b* +28..+59; saffron a*+16.6 b*+60.5; ochre a*+41.6 b*+54.0; madder a*+47.7 b*+38.2 (hue 37–39 — the manuscript red sits at orange-red, never magenta). INK-900 HAS a* +4.4 / hue 8.8 — the shadows are warm-leaning red, never cool-blue. This is the manuscript fact: illumination has no cool shadows.
- The two deliberate cool breaks:
  - VERDIGRIS: a* −40.4, b* +15.3, hue 159 — the ONLY foreground cool. A green that leans olive (positive b*), which is why it survives at the 300-tint (sage #6FB796 hue 162) without going mint-websafe.
  - INDIGO: b* −12.5, hue 289 — blue-violet, the ONLY negative-b* token. Support/shadow only (C 13.3, L* 10.3 — it reads as a cooler black).
- Temperature band rule: WARM = hue 0–100; COOL = hue 140–300. Verdigris clears the warm field by Δhue 70–85 (from gold/saffron) and ~100 (from umber); indigo by Δhue ~200. A break only READS as a break if Δhue ≥ 60 from every warm hue it abuts — both cools satisfy this, which is why they are unambiguous signals.

### 2.2 When a cool may enter the field — the trigger grammar
A cool accent is a RESERVED EVENT, never ambient. It enters on exactly three triggers, and on no other:
1. DATA VALENCE (verdigris): a numeric value must signal positive/healthy. Verdigris is legible (sage on ink 8.3:1) and UNambiguous precisely because it is the only cool in a warm field.
2. DEPTH (indigo): a surface must recede — panel cavity, shadow under panels, the weave's shadow pass. Indigo never carries text (as text it is indistinguishable from ink: 16.2:1 on parchment is ink-like); its job is to make the warm surfaces recede.
3. CONNECTION/LOADING: the verdigris strand in the weave loader (third strand of the trigon).
Cool is never introduced "because it looks nice," never against another pigment (§3), and never in the frame system (frames are gold-on-umber by law).

### 2.3 Max one foreground cool per viewport
verdigris OR indigo as a foreground signal, never both. Indigo-in-background (depth) is exempt but is always muted (C ≤ 13). Verdigris counts as the viewport's one cool and blocks any other. This is the temperature-side twin of the "max two chroma families" rule.

### 2.4 Temperature rides the state ramp (two-channel separation)
Every interaction-state move shifts luminance AND temperature together — never luminance alone — so a deuteranope (who collapses the warm axis) still separates states by temperature where red/green merge: rest→hover = +warmth; rest→pressed = −light, hue browns toward ink's red-lean; rest→dim = chroma collapses to umber. The spec's on-ink substeps already implement this; the theory is WHY: a state ramp that moves only in Y is invisible to a deuteranope on the warm axis.

## §3 SATURATION DISCIPLINE — the saturated-on-soft model

### 3.1 Why pigment-on-neutral, never pigment-on-pigment
A pigment's IDENTITY is carried by its chroma (C 28–68). It is legible as a specific hue only through CHROMA CONTRAST — a saturated figure against a low-chroma ground. Two saturated neighbors do two destructive things at once: (a) simultaneous contrast hue-shifts each (red next to yellow pushes the red cooler, the yellow greener), and (b) figure-ground separation dies. Measured: madder-500 on saffron-500 = 3.20:1 with ΔC only 1.6 (C 61.1 vs 62.7 — neither recedes); gold-500 on saffron-500 = 1.23:1, ΔC 3.2, dE 9.86 — one blob. The manuscript model is literal: pigment is laid on the SOFT vellum or IN the dark field, never on another pigment; gold reads because it sits on vellum, vermilion reads because it sits in a dark outline.

### 3.2 The quantified seating law
- A full-chroma pigment (C ≥ 28) may sit only on a neutral with C ≤ 20 (parchment C 3.2–15, umber C 18.2–19.4, ink C 4.4 — all legal) or on the near-chromaless ink.
- Minimum chroma ratio pigmentC : groundC ≥ 2.5. Checked: madder on parch-300 = 61.1/13.1 = 4.7 ✓; verdi 43.2/13.1 = 3.3 ✓; gold 59.5/13.1 = 4.5 ✓; any pigment on ink = huge ✓. The manuscript neutrals are EXACTLY low enough in chroma to seat every pigment — this self-consistency is what makes "saturated-on-soft" a system and not a taste.
- Never co-seat two pigments. Max one chroma family per viewport (gold always counts as one). If a second pigment is unavoidable: one is demoted below the full-chroma bar — to a 700-step text (C 23–40) or a 300-tint (C 23–28) — and the pair is separated by ≥ 1px neutral (umber-300 #D0B28E) or ≥ 16px air.
- THE FLAG LAW, given its perceptual cause: saffron (hue 74.6) + verdigris (hue 159.3) + madder (hue 38.7) co-present = three full-chroma hues spanning 120° of hue at EQUAL chroma — the loudest possible equal-weight adjacency (tricolor vibration), plus the Ethiopian flag. Perceptual reason and cultural taboo agree; both forbid the trio in one frame.

### 3.3 Full chroma is only "full" where weight allows
Bright warms (gold-500, saffron, parch-gold: Y > 0.28, C > 24) are chroma-thin on the bright wall — they are halation, not figure (§4). So full-chroma-on-parchment is reserved for the dark pigments (madder-500 Y .102, verdi-500 Y .193) and even they appear as marks ≤ 12px; full-chroma-on-ink is reserved for the bright 300-tints. Chroma is a resource spent once per figure.

## §4 VIBRATION THEORY — the dial, not a bug

Vibration (Bezold shimmer / chromatic aberration / simultaneous-contrast halation) is a boundary failure: two hues whose colors differ but whose LUMINANCE is too close for the eye to fixate the edge. The classic trigger is complementary hue at equal luminance; this palette's variant is HALATION — high-chroma warm on a near-same-luminance same-hue field, where the edge "floats."

### 4.1 The contrast-zone dial (measured from the palette)
| zone | boundary contrast | behavior | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| invisible | cr ≤ 1.15 | no boundary formed | never a signal (parch-gold on parch-300 = 1.03:1 — must not be a line on the wall) |
| VIBRATION | 1.15 < cr ≤ 3.0 | eye cannot fixate; edge shimmers/floats | figure must be mark-only and/or separated |
| clean | cr > 3.0 | boundary resolves | the non-text floor (why 3:1 exists) |
| text | cr ≥ 4.5 | AA | with §1.3 weight |

### 4.2 Two vibration regimes with exact conditions
HALATION (float): high-chroma accent on a similar-hue ground — Δhue ≤ 12 AND ΔC ≥ 25 AND cr in the vibration zone. Measured offenders on the wall:
- gold-500 on parch-300: cr 2.21, Δhue 8.6, ΔC 46.4 → HALATION. This is the GILDING SIGNAL — deliberately sanctioned, but rationed by law: ≤ 3% area, marks ≤ 12px, never bulk text (any gold WORD takes gold-700 at 6.3:1).
- saffron-500 on parch-500: cr 1.51, Δhue 10.2, ΔC 47.8 → halation; forbidden as text (2.05:1 on parch-100).
- ochre on parch-300: cr 2.64, Δhue 36.9, ΔC 55.1 → boundary case; hue separation saves it from pure halation but it is still in the vibration zone → marks only (3.02:1 fails AA as text).

COMPLEMENT-SHIMMER: Δhue 120–180 AND |L*Δ| ≤ 20 AND both C ≥ 28. The palette's one true shimmer pair:
- madder-500 vs verdigris-500: cr 1.59, Δhue 120.6, L*Δ 12.7, C 61/43 → genuine red/green shimmer. This is the strongest reason the design separates positive/negative by construction — the only place they meet is the weave loader, where the three strands interlace at fixed z-order and overlap only at the woven-knot instant, with stepped (never blended) geometry channels.

### 4.3 The two named problem pairs, separated with numbers
1. MADDER ON SAFFRON: cr 3.20 (just over the clean floor, under the 4.5 text floor), ΔC 1.6 (equal chroma), L*Δ 33.8, Δhue 36.0. Not complement-shimmer — it is EQUAL-CHROMA WARM ADJACENCY: two full-chroma warms, neither recedes, the boundary reads "injury on jaundice." SEPARATION: never adjacent. If both must exist in a viewport: one is a mark (fill ≤ 12px or 1px rule), the other is 700-step text, with ≥ 1px neutral hairline (umber-300) or ≥ 16px air between. Culturally the bleeding-edge miniature / flag-adjacent warning says the same thing.
2. GOLD ON PARCHMENT: cr 2.21, Δhue 8.6, ΔC 46 → halation (the float). SEPARATION: gold on the wall is stroke/dot/mark only; any gold that must carry information moves one luminance step down to gold-700 (cr 6.28) or moves onto ink (gold-500 7.3:1, pale-gold 11.5:1). Gilding must float; gilding must never carry text.

### 4.4 The metameric warm family (the ΔE collision, generalized)
gold-500 / saffron-500 / parchment-gold / pale-gold all sit at hue 72–92 with Y 0.28–0.80 — a near-collinear a*-b* path. To a deuteranope the entire warm axis collapses (gold/ochre ΔE ≈ 3.0 deutan, the spec's number; gold/saffron are near-metameric even to a trichromat at dE 9.86). LAW: bright warm hues may only carry DIFFERENT ROLES (gold = live/signature, saffron = caution/status, ochre = urgent) and only ONE may be "figure" at a time — never sibling data channels, never co-seated. Role-locking is the anti-collision device; it is not optional.

### 4.5 The separation prescription (general, threshold numbers)
- Any two colors at cr ≤ 3.0 on a shared boundary must be separated by ≥ 1px neutral or a luminance step that lifts the pair ≥ 3:1.
- Any pair with BOTH C ≥ 28 may not share a 1px boundary at all — add ≥ 8px/16px air or a neutral rule.
- Δhue ≤ 12 AND ΔC ≥ 25 AND cr ≤ 3.0 → halation: one of the two must drop to mark-weight or move one Y-step.
- Δhue 120–180 AND |L*Δ| ≤ 20 AND C both ≥ 28 → complement shimmer: never co-seat, ever.
- Vibration is a dial with one sanctioned setting: the gold-on-parchment gilding float, and it is paid for with the 3% cap and the ≤ 12px mark rule.

## §5 EXTENSION RULES — how to add without breaking the physics
1. New pigment: declare hue, weight, temperature. Hue must sit Δhue > 12 from every existing pigment (no near-warm clones); C ≥ 28 for a full-chroma core with a 5-step blend toward parch-300 / ink-900 (never white/black); declare WARM or COOL — if COOL it must REPLACE verdigris or indigo in role (there is no third cool role; max two cool accents total, one foreground).
2. New neutral: C ≤ 20, and must fall into the ground band (L* ≥ 86), the structure band (L* 27–37), or the near-black band (L* ≤ 8). No mid-grays (L* 40–70, C low) — they would fill the empty luminance gaps that make the staircase read.
3. New tint/shade: always the parchment/ink blend law, so it inherits the field's temperature (a shade blended to black would go cool-gray and break the warm world).
4. Legality gate for any new pigment: its 700-step must clear 4.5:1 on parch-300 AND its 300-tint must clear 4.5:1 on ink-900, or it is mark-only.
5. Any encoding needs ≥ 2 channels (hue/temperature/luminance/geometry) — never one.

## §6 HOW HARMONY SURVIVES ACROSS THEMES
The light and dark themes are not different palettes; they are the SAME hue lattice read at a different Y-ordinate. The pigment's HUE is invariant (madder hue 38.7 in both themes); only its WEIGHT changes (500-core on parchment ↔ 300-tint on ink). Temperature is preserved (same warm field, same two cool breaks, same warm-lean ink). Vibration is preserved BY CONSTRUCTION: on parchment the dark pigments are figure (all ≥ 3:1: madder 6.6, verdi 4.1, gold-700 6.3); on ink the bright tints are figure (sage 8.3, red-onink 7.4, saffron-onink 11.7, pale-gold 11.5) — every THEME-LEGAL figure-ground pair clears the 3.0 floor. The forbidden cross (bright core on the bright wall, dark core on the dark panel) is precisely where halation and illegibility live — which is why the two themes assign opposite weights to the same hue, and why a theme flip never produces a vibrating pair. One thing cannot survive the flip: a bright-warm FIGURE on the bright wall — so gold-500 and saffron-500 never carry information on parchment in either theme.

## 1. The wheel grammar — why these hues harmonize, and the two-channel theorem
Approximate hue angles of the pigment cores (HSV): madder ≈8°, ochre ≈21°, umber ≈27°, saffron ≈36°, gold ≈40°, verdigris ≈156°, indigo ≈231°.
- The manuscript palette is NOT six independent hues. It is ONE narrow warm monochromatic wedge (ochre 21° → umber 27° → saffron 36° → gold 40°, roughly 19° wide) PLUS two cool poles at ~156° (verdigris) and ~231° (indigo), with madder (8°) sitting on the warm edge as the rubrication red. Parchment and ink are the two achromatic warm-tinted endpoints of the value axis.
- Harmony formula = ANALOGOUS warm monochromy (the wedge) + exactly ONE complementary pair per composition: gold/saffron ≈116–120° from verdigris, ≈169–195° from indigo, madder≈148° from verdigris. The 60-30-10 is therefore not decoration discipline but wheel geometry: 60/30 are low-chroma value steps (parchment/umber/ink), chroma is reserved for the 10%.
- THEOREM: the palette has exactly TWO independent data-hue channels under both normal and CVD vision. (a) The WARM AXIS (gold+saffron+ochre+umber) — one channel; under deutan/protan it collapses onto a single yellow-brown ramp, so no two warm hues can ever be sibling data channels. (b) The COOL channel — verdigris, madder, indigo — every pair projects ΔE 20–81 apart after simulation (they span the whole cool arc), so any ONE cool hue always separates cleanly from any warm hue (gold500↔verdi500 deutan ΔE 55.6; gold500↔indigo500 69.1; saffron500↔indigo500 81.0).
- Why restraint is provably correct: "gold + at most one cool" is exactly the wheel-safe combination. The two-chroma-family rule is not taste — it is the only arrangement that survives CVD, and it is also the only extension the provenance allows. Extending the system means interpolating MORE LIGHTNESS STEPS inside an existing family (steps 200/400/600/800 by the same 12%/45%/55%/30% blend law toward Parchment 300 / Ink 900, never white/black); it never means adding a new hue, because every hue slot is claimed and a new hue would break both the manuscript provenance and the two-channel partition.

(3) There is no secondary palette: no analogous ±1-hue-step neighbors, so a terminal that needs two "green" series (bid/ask), or the weave-loader's three strands under a two-family budget, has no legal source. (4) Shadows, glows, and fills are ad hoc — no theory that surfaces are REGISTERS of existing pigments (fills = low steps, shadows = indigo dark end, glows = gold light end), zero new hue. Rule-breaking and the decision procedure are likewise absent.

# THE THEORY OF GROWTH — how the Ethiopian palette extends without breaking

All values below were computed from the shipped hexes (sRGB). Nothing is invented; everything is derived.

## 1. THE WHEEL GRAMMAR — why these hues harmonize

Computed hue positions (HSV/HSL hue, degrees): madder 7.5 · ochre 21.0 · saffron 35.8 · gold 40.3 · verdigris 155.6 · indigo 231.4 · parchment #FCF9F3/#F5E9D1 40.0 · umber 500/700 26.8/21.7 · ink #15090B 350.

Three facts that ARE the harmony:
- PARCHMENT AND GOLD ARE ONE HUE RADIUS (40° both). The ground and the signature metal are the same family at different value/chroma. Gold 100→900 is literally "parchment at rising chroma, falling value" — which is why gold hairlines look drawn ON the wall and never fight it, and why gold is allowed everywhere while madder/verdigris are rationed.
- UMBER AND OCHRE ARE ONE HUE RADIUS (~21°). The workhorse structure color is the ochre pigment's dark register. The neutral scale and the accent scale are the same family at different value.
- THE WARM ARC IS ONLY 33° WIDE (7.5°→40.3°), four pigments at ~11° average spacing. Every warm pigment is within a near-monochrome analogous run — any two warm pigments can share a viewport because their separation never exceeds ~33°.

Structural relationships (the grammar):
- Analogous cluster: the warm arc, madder → ochre → saffron → gold.
- Triadic counterpoint: verdigris (155.6°) sits 132° from the arc centroid (23.9°) — a near-exact 120° triadic third. Verdigris is the system's ONE true dissonance, which is exactly why it is reserved for data valence and is forbidden as decoration.
- Complementary pair: gold (40.3°) ↔ indigo (231.4°) is 11° from exact opposition (220.3°) — the ONLY true complementarity, and it is the gilding/depth axis: gold glows, indigo grounds. This pair is why shadows are indigo and glows are gold.
- madder is the arc's alarm edge; gold the luminous terminal; ochre the structural center (≈umber); saffron is gold's cautionary neighbor at ΔE 9.9 — too close for sibling channels, hence the role-lock.

Combination rules FROM the wheel:
1. "Max two chroma families" restated in hue terms: any viewport's chroma = {warm arc} + ≤1 other. All warm pigments count as ONE family (they fit in 33°). The second seat is verdigris OR indigo-as-visible-accent — never both; indigo-as-shadow is value-work and doesn't count.
2. Temperature pairing: pair warm↔cool, or warm↔warm within the arc. Never two cool accents (the system has one cool seat).
3. Gold is the universal bridge (arc terminal + metallic neutral + same radius as the ground). When in doubt, substitute gold for the second chroma.
4. Forbidden: gold↔saffron/ochre as SIBLING data channels (measured ΔE gold↔saffron 9.9); the madder↔verdigris pair only as a valence pair (up/down) with mandatory geometry; the saffron+verdigris+madder trio never co-present (flag law).

## 2. THE WARM-NEUTRAL DERIVATION LAW (the formal method)

FORMAL. In gamma-encoded sRGB (the same interpolation as CSS color-mix(in srgb)), the blend axis between core pigment P and anchor A is S(t) = t·P + (1−t)·A on the 8-bit values, t∈[0,1]. Anchors are the two warm poles — A_light = Parchment 300 #F5E9D1 (hue 40°, L* 92.7, relChroma 0.079); A_dark = Ink 900 #15090B (hue 350°, L* 3.4, relChroma 0.400 — a RED-LEANING near-black, not a neutral!).

CANONICAL STEPS (verified — this reproduces every token in the existing spec exactly, all six pigments): 100 = t 0.12 (12% pigment + 88% parchment) · 300 = t 0.45 · 500 = t 1.00 (core) · 700 = t 0.55 (55% pigment + 45% ink) · 900 = t 0.30. E.g. madder → #ECD3BC/#D19580/#A62F1E/#651E15/#401411, matching the shipped table.

THE PERCEPTUAL READING (this is the "why"): the fixed t-values land at nearly identical L* fractions across all six pigments — 100 ≈ 0.88 of the way from core to parchment in L*, 300 ≈ 0.53, 700 ≈ 0.43 of the way from core to ink, 900 ≈ 0.69 (measured 0.869–0.892 / 0.523–0.548 / 0.416–0.443 / 0.673–0.713 for madder/ochre/saffron/gold/verdigris). The system fixes ONE global t and the perceptual evenness is emergent — no per-pigment tuning. The one outlier confirms the law: indigo (a dark pigment) deviates (its 900 step sits at L*-fraction 0.79 and collapses toward ink) — that is the built-in signal that indigo is a shadow pigment, never a foreground accent.

WHY WARM ANCHORS PRESERVE CHROMA AND WHITE/BLACK KILLS IT — the chroma-floor theorem:
- Mixing toward achromatic white/black sets a ZERO chroma floor and an UNDEFINED hue destination; the mixture collapses toward the L axis (gray) and its hue is hostage to the pigment's raw direction.
- Mixing toward a CHROMATIC anchor sets a floor equal to the anchor's own chroma at the anchor's weight, and a destination equal to the anchor's hue.
- Measured: saffron 300 toward parchment = #EFCA8E, relChroma 0.255, hue 37.1°; the same 45% toward white = #F5D6A8, relChroma 0.186. Parchment blend retains 0.44× of core chroma vs white's 0.32× (37% more), and its pale end GLOWS golden instead of graying to eggshell. Madder 300 toward parchment = #D19580, a warm terracotta at hue 15.6° (on the arc); toward white = #D7A19A, a desaturated pink.
- The dark end is the same story: ink carries relChroma 0.40 at hue 350°, so even a 70%-ink step (900) keeps a deep warm maroon/bronze cast and never lands on neutral black — that is why every 900 in the table is a dark red-brown, not a gray.
- CONVERGENCE GUARANTEE: every blend axis ends on the warm light pole (40°) or the red-lean dark pole (350°). Every color in the system therefore lies within a warm hue band of <52°, so random samplings of the palette still harmonize. The system is an attractor with two warm fixed points — this is the mathematical reason it "can't go wrong."

EXTENSION WITHOUT BREAKING:
- Even steps by t-bisection (verified hexes): 200 = t 0.28 · 400 = t 0.72 · 600 = t 0.78 · 800 = t 0.42. E.g. gold: 200 #E9D2A3 · 400 #D5AD5C · 600 #A17726 · 800 #61441A; verdigris: 200 #B9CEB1 · 400 #5AA57E · 600 #1C6E4C · 800 #193F2E.
- Arbitrary step: choose the desired L* fraction λ of the core→anchor range, solve t, or linearly interpolate t between the two bracketing canonical values (near-equivalent).
- THE MEMBERSHIP TEST (trace-back): a new color BELONGS iff it is expressible as S(t) = t·P + (1−t)·A with P ∈ {the six pigments}, A ∈ {Parchment300, Ink900}, t ∈ [0,1] — i.e., its RGB sits on one of the system's 12 blend axes — OR it is one of the two named exception ramps (alert-red lift §5; pale gold #E5C193). Verify by checking the candidate RGB is a convex combination of exactly one pigment and one anchor. Everything else is not the system.
- Three gates on any extension: (i) hue inside a seat — warm arc 7–52° ∪ verdigris 143–168° ∪ indigo 219–243° (the outer bounds of the §3 neighbors); (ii) if it must be a distinguishable data channel, ΔE ≥ 8 from co-resident series; (iii) never ΔE < 6 from a role-locked sibling (gold/saffron/ochre).

The step discipline (unnumbered steps forbidden) has ONE exception: the ALERT RED LIFT. WHY MADDER, AND ONLY MADDER:
1. Rubrication — the source tradition already sanctions red as the one voice that cuts through the text; the lift modernizes rubrication's rhetorical role. It is a semantic act, not decoration.
2. Red is universal alarm; the lift is the alarm growing louder.
3. Wheel position: madder is the arc's boundary (7.5°). Lifting it moves toward ochre (21°) — itself a warning hue — so the lift stays inside warning semantics. BOUNDS (verified):
- HUE stays in the madder zone 0–15°: lifted rest #E8836F measures hue 9.9°, L* 65.4. It never crosses into ochre's 21°.

## 6. THE DECISION TREE — given any new requirement, pick a color that belongs

STEP 0 — DO YOU NEED COLOR AT ALL? Frames, dividers, muted text, borders → umber/parchment/ink first (the workhorse register). Only a semantic state, a data valence, a live indicator, or an illustration surface enters the 10% budget. Not in the 10% → it's a neutral.

STEP 1 — WHAT ROLE? (role → locked family): positive/tick-up → verdigris · negative/tick-down/alert → madder · caution → saffron / urgent → ochre (mutually exclusive, pick one) · live/existence → gold · neutral/disabled → umber/parchment · support/depth/shadow → indigo · prose gloss → saffron (one-per-article underline). A requirement whose role has no family is rejected, not the palette.

STEP 2 — WHICH STEP? (the theme mapping IS the step selection): text on parchment → 700 · text/fill on ink → 300-tint · fill/dot ≤12px → 500 core · wash/glow/shadow → 100/900 @ alpha · hairline/rule → umber 300–800 or gold rules. This single rule IS the theme-invariance law: light↔dark is a step-mirror about 500 (700↔300-tint), never a hue change — the same painting at different exposure.

STEP 3 — WITHIN THE BUDGET? Max two chroma families per viewport, gold counts as one. The new color must BE one of the current two or REPLACE one. A viewport already at gold + verdigris cannot add saffron without an eviction.

STEP 4 — MEMBERSHIP TEST (trace-back): candidate RGB must be a weighted average of exactly one pigment + one anchor (Parchment300 or Ink900), or a named exception. If not expressible as t·P + (1−t)·A, derive it: pigment by role (Step 1), t by L* target (Step 2), compute the blend.

STEP 5 — THE THREE GATES: (i) hue inside a seat; (ii) as a data channel, ΔE ≥ 8 from co-resident series and ≥ 6 from role-locked siblings; (iii) no forbidden adjacencies (gold↔saffron/ochre as siblings; the pigment trio; the green-yellow-red flag).

STEP 6 — NAME AND LOCK IT: extensions enter the token table with a role-locked name and a restraint note (where allowed, where forbidden). Extensions don't float free.

FAIL-CLOSED: a candidate that clears Steps 0–3 but fails 4–5 falls back to umber — or it doesn't ship.

## 7. HOW HARMONY SURVIVES IN DATA AND ACROSS THEMES

IN DATA: channels separate by HUE first (family), then by step (value) — never by value alone. Within-family siblings (the §3 secondary palette) carry a mandatory geometry channel because 12° of hue is below deutan discrimination at 11–13px. The verdigris/madder valence pair is the one permitted warm–cool dissonance, always paired with sign+glyph+underline (never color alone). Sibling series come from different families wherever possible.

ACROSS THEMES: theme-invariance is a step-mirror, not a re-palette. Every hue, neighbor, shadow base, glow base, and the alert lift's hue are FIXED; only the step register flips (700↔300-tint about 500). Two deliberate asymmetries, both principled: the alert-red lift exists only on ink (light already pierces with deep madder); shadows (light) invert to outlines (dark). Because the hue distribution — warm arc + one cool seat + indigo depth — is invariant, both themes read as the same manuscript seen under different light.

# LIGHT/DARK HARMONY THEORY — the two themes as one harmony system

Governing claim: the two themes are ONE painting under two illuminations. The palette is not a list of colors; it is a set of CHORDS from each pigment to the two manuscript neutrals. The light theme samples every chord at its ink-ward end; the dark theme samples the same chords at their parchment-ward end. Because the chords, not the values, are the identity, the harmony survives theme inversion by construction.

## 0. THE WHEEL GRAMMAR — why these hues harmonize (the missing foundation)

Hue geography (CIELCH h° / L* / C*, D65, computed — use these, not HSV, for separation rules):

- Madder #A62F1E: h39 L38 C61 · Ochre #E46F30: h52 L60 C68 · Saffron #E8A33D: h75 L72 C63 · Deep gold #C9962E: h81 L65 C59 · Pale gold #E5C193: h77 L80 C29 · Umber #6C523D: h65 L37 C18 · Parchment 300 #F5E9D1: h89 L93 C13 · Ink 900 #15090B: h9 L3 C4 · Verdigris #1E8A5E: h159 L51 C43 · Indigo #181B2D: h289 L10 C13.

LAW 0.1 — The warm-band bracket. The two manuscript neutrals are NOT neutral: parchment is yellow (h89), ink is red (h9). Every warm pigment — madder (39), ochre (52), umber (55-65), saffron (75), gold (77-81) — sits on the 80° arc INSIDE that bracket. The palette is a continuous warm sweep from ink-red through parchment-yellow; cohesion is literal adjacency on the wheel. This is why the palette can never feel "unrelated" no matter how it is combined.

LAW 0.2 — The two cool excursions. Verdigris (h159) and indigo (h289) are the only hues outside the warm band, and both are demoted by role: verdigris is the palette's ONE true complement — the near-opposite of madder (121° separation) — and is used only to say "opposite" (positive vs negative, up vs down); indigo is allowed only at shadow depth (L10, C13), never as an accent, so the gold-violet clash the wheel would otherwise permit never occurs. The violet-blue region of the wheel is CORDONED OFF for accents by law: any hue h∈[200°,300°] is shadow-only. This is what keeps a manuscript palette from degrading into a rainbow.

LAW 0.3 — The three relationship types. The entire grammar is three moves, nothing else: (a) the ANALOGOUS chain (madder→ochre→saffron→gold→parchment — used for hierarchy within a family, separated by luminance not hue); (b) the ONE COMPLEMENT pair (madder↔verdigris — used for binary opposition in data); (c) the DEMOTED complement (indigo — shadow/depth only). There is no triadic, no split-complement, no tetradic move in the system; adding one would break restraint.

EXTENSION RULE (the "where can a new hue sit" test): a proposed new pigment is legal iff (1) its core sits inside the warm bracket h∈[9°,89°] OR is the verdigris complement h≈155-165°, OR is shadow indigo; (2) it is ≥90° CIELCH from every existing foreground pigment at its intended use; (3) it is assigned exactly one semantic role (its hue band may not overlap a role-locked neighbor). A proposed data channel must additionally pass the separation law in §8.

## 1. THE MANUSCRIPT DILUTION LAW (the mixing law under everything)

All value change — tints, shades, light-theme darks, dark-theme lifts — is DILUTION toward the two chromatic manuscript neutrals, never toward achromatic white/black. sRGB-linear blend: T = lerp(P, N, α), N ∈ {Parchment 300 #F5E9D1, Ink 900 #15090B}, with the spec's fixed ratios for the 5-step scales.

Why this preserves harmony: parchment (h89, C13) and ink (h9, C4) are themselves chromatic and lie at the two ends of the warm band, so every dilution chord runs THROUGH warm CIELAB space (a*+, b*+). A blend of two chromatic colors stays off the gray core and keeps C* relative to luminance high. Achromatic dilution (toward white or black, a*=b*=0) drags every color through the gray core: saturation collapses per unit luminance, the warm hue is wiped toward gray-green/brown, and the result reads "websafe" (the mint/coral/pale-lemon failure). VERIFIED: the rejected #7BC9A8 sits ΔE≈6.5 off the verdi→parchment chord; the accepted sage #6FB796 is on it. The law is the difference between a pigment and a pastel.

THEME COROLLARY — chord-slicing. Because the light-theme 700/900 steps are lerp toward ink and the dark-theme 300-tints are lerp toward parchment, the two themes are two SAMPLES of the same chords, in opposite directions. A pigment's identity is the chord; its value in a theme is the slice. This is the formal reason the two themes are one system.

## 2. THE INVERSION PRINCIPLE — luminance inverts, hue-family survives

DELIVERABLE 1. Statement: the theme transform (a) inverts the luminance FIELD — ground and near-ground swap: light-theme ground Parchment (L93) ↔ dark-theme ground Ink (L3), and light-theme panel Ink (L3) ↔ dark-theme panel lifted to L6 with light text (L93); (b) preserves hue-family — every pigment stays on its chord, only its slice changes; (c) preserves luminance-ORDER — within the accent set the ordering is identical in both themes (gold lightest, saffron next, verdigris mid, madder darkest: L80/81, L72-81, L51-69, L38-66); (d) preserves the anchor colors — both themes use the SAME parchment and ink as dilution endpoints, so the palette's poles never move.

Why this preserves harmony: harmony is a set of stable RELATIONS — hue angles, temperature order, chroma hierarchy, relative luminance order, complementary oppositions. The Ethiopian transform is luminance-monotonic (order preserved), hue-preserving (wheel geometry fixed by the warm-band bracket), and anchor-preserving (same parchment/ink poles). A transform that leaves every relation invariant cannot break the harmony it transforms. The two themes do not merely "look related"; they share the same relational structure by construction. The dark theme is not a second palette — it is the light palette re-sliced.

HUE-DRIFT BUDGET: a manuscript-pass lift may move a pigment's CIELCH hue up to ±15° from its core (it must stay inside its family band), and must never cross into a neighbor's band or the gray core. Madder-coral drifts h39→38; verdigris-sage h159→162; both legal. A lift that pushed madder to pink (h0) or saffron to lemon (h100) is out-of-family and rejected.

## 3. DARK-GROUND HARMONY — gold-threads-over-darkness; the on-ink accent theory

DELIVERABLE 2. The dark theme is the manuscript-at-night: parchment darkens to near-black, ink recedes into the wall, and the GOLD LEAF and lighter pigments catch the lamp. Physically, gold leaf is reflective — in lamplight it reads BRIGHTER than the darkened parchment. So on-ink pigments are not "lightened cores"; they are the pigments caught by light, and the lift is a change of MATERIAL READING from body-color paint (absorbing, dark) to caught-light (luminous, bright).

THE LIFT FUNCTION. on-ink accent = the blend of the 500-core toward Parchment 300 that (1) clears 4.5:1 on the panel ground, (2) keeps C* above the umber ceiling, (3) keeps hue within the family band (±15°, LAW 2), (4) is a SINGLE value per pigment so both themes share one pigment identity. VERIFIED against the computed contrast floor — the reason the lift exists is NOT chiefly contrast: on ink, gold core 7.3:1, saffron 9.1:1, ochre 6.1:1 all pass; only madder 2.8:1 and verdigris 4.5:1 fail. Every pigment lifts anyway, because a luminance-passing CORE on umber sits in "paint register" (L60-72) fighting the text register, and reads as a colored blob on a dark wall rather than as pigment catching light. The lift moves all accents UP into the same light band as the text (L66-81), so accents and text share one luminance plane — the lit surface of the manuscript.

LAW 3.1 — Why lifted pastels work on umber. On a dark WARM ground, the eye adapts dark-and-warm, and the visible discrimination of an accent from the ground is carried by CHROMA, not just luminance — because the warm accents are hue-close to the umber ground (gold h77 vs umber h55-65). Separation therefore requires the accent to hold C* ABOVE the umber ceiling. Ceiling: umber C*≈18-25 (umber 500 C18; raised card C9). Floor for on-ink accents: C*≥29 — sage C32, pale gold C29, coral C43, saffron C49 all clear it.

LAW 3.2 — The warm chroma must not grayscale. If an on-ink warm accent is whitened (white-lift) its chroma collapses toward the umber family and it becomes indistinguishable from the ground — the glow dies and the panel flattens to brown-on-brown. Quantified: a legal lift must keep C* above the ceiling while gaining luminance; the parchment-blend achieves this because parchment is itself chromatic (C13, h89), so the dilution chord stays off the gray core. White (a*=b*=0) is the one forbidden dilution because it is exactly the path that grayscales.

WHY THE MINT→SAGE FIX IS REAL (computed): #7BC9A8 (L75, h164) vs #6FB796 (L69, h162) differ by ΔE≈6.5 and, critically, the mint is LIGHTER (L75) and more cyan (a*−32) — it floated above the other accents and read websafe. The sage pulls lightness down to the accent band and warms b*, so it reads olive-copper (the actual verdigris pigment), not mint. The fix is 80% a lightness re-seat + 20% a chroma re-tilt — a color-family correction, not a taste call.

## 4. GLOW VS SHADOW — gold as light-emitter, indigo as shadow; the single glow rule

DELIVERABLE 3. LAWS:

LAW 4.1 — Material hierarchy: metal vs dye. Gold is the METAL; it is the manuscript's light source. The pigments (madder, verdigris, saffron) are DYES; they absorb and reflect gently. Glow — halo, bloom, emission, self-illumination — is a MATERIAL property, not a brightness property. LUMINOSITY (brightness) is available to any pigment at its 300-tint; GLOW is gold's alone. Consequence: only gold may carry a halo token (#F0DFBD @0.85 behind the eye-dot), only gold may exceed the one-glow budget. If saffron or verdigris glowed, the metal/dye hierarchy collapses and the manuscript becomes a dashboard.

LAW 4.2 — The single glow rule. One glow per viewport (equivalently: one light source per scene, like one lamp over a manuscript). Gold is that source. On dark, gold is an EMITTER: pale gold #E5C193 at L80 with the C29 chroma held so it stays yellow, never khaki (Law 3.2). On light, gold is a MARK: the 500 core #C9962E at L65 is an amber pigment against bright parchment — a drawn line, not a light. The two golds are the same metal seen as emitter (dark) and as pigment (light).

LAW 4.3 — Indigo as shadow; the chiaroscuro. On the light theme, parchment is warm light; its shadows must be COOL, or they read as mud. Indigo #181B2D (h289, L10) is the cool shadow — panel recesses, depth under paintings, drop-shadow tinting — because warm-advances/cool-recedes (aerial perspective): warm light pops off the field, cool dark recedes into it. On the dark theme the ground is already near-black, so there is no deeper; depth is carried instead by gold LIGHT (the frame, the threads). THE DEPTH-MECHANISM COMPLEMENTARITY: each theme's depth engine is the complement of its ground — on warm parchment, depth = cool shadow (indigo); on near-black, depth = warm light (gold). This is the same polarity inverted, and it is why the dark theme needs no indigo.

LAW 4.4 — Shadow discipline. Indigo is the ONLY shadow tint in the light theme; no black shadows (neutral-black on warm parchment grays to green-mud). Shadow sources ≤1 per scene, matching the single glow. On dark, shadows vanish entirely — indigo merges into the ink family; the palette's shadow and its ground become the same material, and depth moves to line-work (frames, hairlines).

## 5. THE PANEL-IN-DARK PROBLEM — re-voicing the painting on a near-black wall

DELIVERABLE 4. The problem: light-theme panels are ink 900 #15090B; a dark-theme wall at the same depth makes panels vanish. The resolution is a re-voice, not a re-color:

LAW 5.1 — The chrome stack collapses to a near-black family. In the dark theme, wall, panel, and raised card all live within L*≈2-9 and within ~1.1:1 of each other (verified: wall #0D0508 vs ink #15090B = 1.0:1; panel #1B1114 vs wall = 1.09:1; panel vs raised card umber 900 #241410 = 1.04:1). ALL figure-ground is carried by line-work — the gold frame, hairlines, and mat — never by luminance contrast. This is the gallery-at-night metaphor made literal: a gilt frame on a dark wall, where the frame, not the canvas edge, is what the eye sees.

LAW 5.2 — Why the panel lifts to #1B1114 at all. Not for visible lightness (it stays ~1.1:1) but to (a) give the pale-gold frame a marginally lighter SEAT than the wall, so the frame reads as the panel's edge and not the wall's; (b) seat the parchment-700 hairline/mat; (c) hold the panel a perceptible warm step (3× the wall's relative luminance, ~4 L*) above the wall so the wall+panel remain one receding mass while the panel still "lifts" off it. The lift is a warm-weighted PERCEPTUAL step, not a contrast step.

LAW 5.3 — The frame is the figure-ground carrier. Pale gold #E5C193 clears ~11.9:1 on the deep-umber wall and ~10.9:1 on the lifted panel (verified) — roughly 100× the panel's luminance. Frame luminance ≫ panel ≫ wall, in that strict order; the frame is a light source relative to the panel, the panel is material relative to the wall.

LAW 5.4 — Hierarchy floors. The panel must stay a full value-step below the raised cards (#241410) so content hierarchy survives, and the muted on-panel text (parchment 900 #9C7C55 = 4.8:1 on the panel, verified) must keep clearing 4.5:1. The resolved two-depth ground system (this resolves the token-vs-frame inconsistency in the current spec): general canvas = ink 900 #15090B with raised cards umber 900 #241410; the terminal/gallery view drops the wall to deep umber #0D0508 with panels lifted to #1B1114. The invariant is the same in both: any painting/well is darker than its raised siblings, ≥1 warm step above a deep-umber wall, and its boundary is carried by the gold frame.

## 6. THEME PARITY — the same system in both themes, no invented colors

DELIVERABLE 5. Five testable invariants:

P1 SEMANTIC-HUE INVARIANCE — each semantic role maps to the same pigment family in both themes: positive=verdigris, negative=madder, alert=saffron-family (ochre=urgent, saffron=caution, mutually exclusive), live=gold, neutral=umber, disabled=umber-ward. No role changes hue between themes.

P2 VALUE-TRANSFORM INVARIANCE — each pigment's theme-pair is one chord-slice: light value = ink-ward (700/500), dark value = parchment-ward (300-tint). Same dilution function, opposite direction. Any token that is not provably on its pigment→neutral chord is an invented color.

P3 LUMINANCE-ORDER INVARIANCE — the accent set's luminance order is identical in both themes, so the eye reads the same hierarchy in each (gold lightest, saffron, verdigris, madder darkest).

P4 PROPORTION INVARIANCE — the 60-30-10 holds STRUCTURALLY in both themes. The 60-30-10 is an area × luminance-momentum grammar, not a hue grammar: 60% = rest ground (parchment family on light; ink/umber family on dark), 30% = the structural counterpoint (the dark paintings + umber chrome on light; the light text hierarchy + deeper-than-wall wells on dark), 10% = accent (gold + at most one pigment; gold ≤3%; max two chroma families per viewport). The proportion bands survive inversion because the counterpoint material flips with the ground — chiaroscuro mirrored.

P5 THE NO-INVENTED-COLOR TEST (the load-bearing rule). A token T is legal iff ∃ pigment P, neutral N ∈ {parchment 100/300, ink 900/950, umber 900}, α∈[0,1]: T = sRGB_lerp(P, N, α) within ΔE≤1.0. Enforced at design time for every token in BOTH themes. Worked examples: sage #6FB796 = verdi 500 55%→parchment 300 = ON-chord, legal. #7BC9A8 = websafe mint = OFF-chord (ΔE≈6.5 from the chord), REJECTED. Every light-theme 700-step = lerp toward ink 900 = on-chord. A dark theme where every color passes P5 is, by construction, not a second palette — it is the same palette re-sliced. This is the guarantee behind "no theme where a color is invented."

## 7. HARMONY IN DATA — how the wheel governs sibling channels

The wheel grammar, not a taste rule, decides what may sit together as data:

LAW 7.1 — Sibling data channels must be separated on hue OR luminance (deutans collapse the red-green axis; the yellow-blue axis and the luminance axis survive). Two channels are legal siblings iff they differ ≥90° in CIELCH hue OR ≥1 full luminance step (≥3:1 between them) — and never if both are in the warm band at near-equal luminance.

LAW 7.2 — Why gold/ochre can never be sibling channels (derived, not asserted): gold h81/L65 vs ochre h52/L60 — hue-close (28°), near-equal luminance (ΔL*≈5), both inside the warm band. For a deuteranope they collapse to one brown; for a normal observer they read as one channel (no separation). The existing ΔE-3.0 citation is the deutan-relevant reading of exactly this failure. Forbidden.

LAW 7.3 — Why madder/verdigris CAN be the up/down pair: they are the palette's one true complement (121° CIELCH hue separation) PLUS a 13-L* luminance offset PLUS a mandatory geometric channel (the signed glyph, the wash direction — color never carries sign alone). Two independent axes of separation + a third non-color channel. This is why the binary market opposition rides the wheel's natural opposition.

LAW 7.4 — Within a hue family, only one channel may speak (the analogous chain is a HIERARCHY, not a set of siblings): saffron and ochre are role-locked (caution vs urgent, one per viewport) precisely because they are 23° apart and would otherwise collide; gold and saffron are role-locked (gold = live/signature, saffron = status text). The 60-30-10 and the "two chroma families per viewport" rule are the same law at composition scale.

## Deepened elements


### Forbidden hue zone (empty half of the wheel) — hard boundary + extension admission filter

BOUNDARY — the hard line. For every candidate compute h = hue_ab = atan2(b,a) in D65 CIELAB, C = C* = √(a²+b²). REJECT if ANY holds: (i) 180° ≤ h ≤ 270° AND C ≥ 25 — the forbidden sector, closed inclusive so no gray edge exists; (ii) guardbands 170° ≤ h < 180° OR 270° < h ≤ 280° at C > 25 — blocks a "verdigris-like" teal (172°, C35) or a violet-slate (275°, C30) from flanking past verdigris (159.3°) or indigo (289.1°); (iii) C above family ceiling — warm ≤ 68 (ochre-orange 68.2 is the system max), cool ≤ 43 (verdigris 43.2). Indigo stays legal only because h = 289.1 ∉ [180,270] and C = 13.3 < 25; sage (162.0°, C31.7) only because h < 170. Reference, not entry: the boundary is why madder's complement (218.7°) and verdigris's (339.3°) are deliberate absences, not accidents.

THE 4-GATE FILTER — a new hue passes ALL or it dies.
G1 ZONE: satisfy the boundary AND land in a named allowed zone: warm wedge 38.7°–91°, counterpoint band 150°–170°, or shadow slot 285°–295° at C ≤ 20. No hue outside these three has an address.
G2 SECOND-CHANNEL LAW: against every existing channel hue, at least one of Δhue ≥ 25°, ΔL ≥ 15, ΔC ≥ 20 must hold (the §6 rule; CVD-safe by construction).
G3 BLEND: tints toward parchment 300 #F5E9D1, shades toward ink 900 #15090B — never white/black (identical to the 5-step derivation).
G4 INTRODUCTION: one role, one step, <1% of any viewport, and only after a value/chroma extension of an existing pigment has failed. Max-two-families and the flag rule (§4.3) apply on top.

THE LADDER — extend in order; stop at first success.
L1 VALUE: new step of an existing pigment; Δhue < 5° from parent core; never approaches the boundary.
L2 CHROMA: desat variant; Δhue < 5° from parent, C ≥ 15 below parent core, ΔL ≥ 10; re-run G2 vs siblings.
L3 HUE: a genuinely new hue — runs all 4 gates; the only rung that can fail the boundary.

DELIBERATE USE OF THE EMPTINESS.
THE COOL RESERVE: the counterpoint band holds at most TWO hue families — the verdigris family (verdigris 500 + sage 300) + at most ONE admitted cool. A future state (e.g. archival/offline) may admit exactly one legal cool outside verdigris: h ∈ 150°–170°, C 25–35, L* 48–58, blended to parchment; must be ≥ 15 ΔL from sage (#6FB796, L69.1 — L52 clears by 17) and ≥ 25° hue from any other channel; must hold the 5° theme-invariance budget (light/dark hue_ab differ < 5°). It reads as verdigris-at-rest, never a new hue.
THE SHADOW CLOSE: 285°–295° is only 10° wide; indigo (289.1°) occupies it, and the ≥ 25° hue rule makes a second shadow impossible by arithmetic — the cool family is provably CLOSED at two. The forbidden sector enforces this: it fences the cool region so a third cool has no legal address; the only remaining space is the 150°–170° band, which IS the reserve.

VERIFY. Probe every admitted hex: convert sRGB→linear→D65 XYZ→Lab, compute h/C, assert G1–G4, and assert AA ≥ 4.5:1 on its ground if it carries text — verdi 500 is 4.1 (fails), sage core is 8.3 (passes); on-ink 300-tints must also hold ≥ 4.5:1.


### Cross-theme harmony invariance

P0 PRINCIPLE (stated): the grammar — warm wedge, cool counterpoint, empty zone, triad, Second-Channel Law — is identical in both themes. A theme flip changes L and C arbitrarily; it may change hue_ab by strictly less than 5° per token. A token that violates the budget breaks the grammar, not just the color.

P1 PER-TOKEN BUDGET (measured): |h_light − h_dark| < 5.0° for every shared token. Verified: gold 80.7°/pale-gold 77.0° = 3.7° ✓; madder 38.7°/onink-red 37.1° = 1.6° ✓; verdigris 159.3°/sage 162.0° = 2.7° ✓; umber 55–65° in both themes ✓; saffron 74.6°/onink-saffron 87.7° = 13.1° — FAIL.

P2 FIX onink-saffron: set hue_ab to 78.5° (±1.0°) → drift 3.9°, inside budget. Mechanism (hue-constrained blend): the dark theme lifts accents toward parchment (pole ≈ 88°). Saffron→parchment travels 13.4° of hue, so cap the parchment tint fraction at α_max = min(1, 5°/|h_pole − h_token|) = 5.0/13.4 = 0.37. Lift beyond α_max must use achromatic white (L-only), never more parchment. General rule: parchment pole (≈88°) governs dark-theme tints, ink pole (8.8°) governs light-theme shades; the 5° budget is spent on the hue-committed fraction only, all remaining rescale is L,C-only.

P3 WARM BOOKENDING (why it holds): both grounds are hue-committed and sit on the warm arc — parchment 85–91° (chroma 3–15, the wedge's pale end), ink 8.8° (chroma 4.4, red-lean). The poles bracket the wedge 38.7°→91° plus the red flank 0–38.7°, so every ground-blend moves an accent along the warm arc, never toward the empty zone 180°–270°. A neutral or blue-black ground would let tints slide cool and break the wedge; ink's 8.8° red-lean is the specific guard. A P1 violation is always a blend that left the wedge; P2's cap is the enforcement.

P4 CROSS-THEME LEGEND TEST — all four must pass. (1) Per-token drift: recompute P1; fail ≥ 5°. (2) Pairwise preservation: for each legal sibling pair present in both themes, |Δh_pair(light) − Δh_pair(dark)| ≤ 5°; a ≥25° hue separation in light must stay ≥20° in dark. (3) Zone/order integrity: no hue enters 180°–270° at chroma > 25 in either theme; token hue_ab order is identical in both themes (no theme-dependent hue swap). (4) Role-lock survival: after P2, verify ΔE2000(pale-gold 77.0°, onink-saffron 78.5°) ≥ 12; if below, lower pale-gold chroma 28.7 → ~20 (one step toward parchment-tan) until ΔC ≥ 25 separates them, and confirm onink-saffron still meets its on-ink contrast floor (≥ 4.5:1 at its text size).

P5 ADMISSION FILTER (invariance clause): a new token's light and dark values are admitted as one pair — both must pass P1, P4(2)–(3), and respect P2's tint cap before the token is legal in either theme.

### Vibration & halation regimes — the separation dial

**0. Derivation.** Vibration is never a stored token — it is a derived quantity recomputed for every boundary pair at adjacency time from five measured inputs: WCAG contrast ratio cr; CIELAB Δhue, ΔC, |L*Δ|; and C_min = min(C_a, C_b). Fixed zones: invisible cr ≤ 1.15 · vibration 1.15 < cr ≤ 3.0 · clean cr > 3.0 · text cr ≥ 4.5.

**1. The adjacency checker (evaluate top-down; first hit wins).** For each pair a/b sharing a boundary:
- **NO-TOUCH — complement-shimmer:** Δhue ∈ [120°,180°] ∧ |L*Δ| ≤ 20 ∧ C_min ≥ 28 → forbidden forever; no size, opacity, or separation remedy. Madder-500 (hue 38.7, C 61.1) vs verdigris-500 (hue 159.3, C 43.2): cr 1.59, Δhue 120.6, L*Δ 12.7 → hits. Legal co-presence only in the weave loader, where pigment surfaces never touch (fixed z-order, stepped 120ms over/under passes).
- **MUST-DROP-WEIGHT — halation:** Δhue ≤ 12 ∧ ΔC ≥ 25 ∧ cr ≤ 3.0 → one party drops to mark-weight (fill ≤12px, 1–2px stroke) or moves one luminance step so the pair clears 3.0. Gold-500 vs parch-300: cr 2.21, Δhue 8.6, ΔC 46.4. Saffron-500 vs parch-500: cr 1.51, Δhue 10.2, ΔC 47.8.
- **NEVER-ADJACENT — equal-chroma warms:** Δhue ∈ [30°,60°] ∧ ΔC < 10 ∧ C_min ≥ 28. Madder-500 vs saffron-500: cr 3.20, ΔC 1.6, L*Δ 33.8, Δhue 36.0. If forced: one becomes 700-step text, the other mark, ≥1px umber-300 hairline or ≥16px air.
- **MUST-SEPARATE — sub-clean:** cr ≤ 3.0 → ≥1px neutral rule or one Y-step lifting the pair ≥3.0. Ochre vs parch-300: cr 2.64, Δhue 36.9, ΔC 55.1 — boundary case; marks-only (3.02:1 < 4.5).
- **MUST-SEPARATE — co-chroma:** C_min ≥ 28 → never a 1px boundary; ≥8px air (16px preferred) or a neutral rule.

**2. Sanctioned vibration — the gilding float.** Gold-on-parchment halation is legal under all four conditions: (a) cr ∈ [1.15, 3.0] (measured 2.21); (b) marks ≤12px or 1px strokes only — gold text is gold-700 at 6.3:1; (c) identity cap 3% area; (d) gold is the viewport's single chroma family. **3. No-touch / must-separate threshold table.** Rows: complement-shimmer (Δhue 120–180, |L*Δ| ≤20, C_min ≥28) → no-touch, no remedy. At the text register Y 0.35–0.57 it collapses further: gold 20.4, madder 19.2, verdi 18.1 — inside the umber ceiling band (18–25), i.e. chroma-indistinguishable from the warm ground (brown-on-brown). White's achromatic endpoint (C=0) anchors no chroma: at every Y where the parchment chord clears 29, white already sits below it.

3. LIGHTNESS RE-SEAT (websafe → illuminated). A candidate pastel Q is legal iff |h(Q)−h_P| ≤ 15°, C*(Q) ≥ 29, and ΔE76(Q, A) ≤ 2.0; otherwise discard Q and adopt A. Mint→sage mechanism: #7BC9A8 (L75.3, C32.8, h164, a−31.5) vs #6FB796 (L69.1, C31.7, h162) gives ΔE = 6.46, of which ΔL = 6.26 (94% of the variance) — the repair is re-seating lightness DOWN 6.3 L* into the accent register plus a warm re-tilt (da −1.4, db −0.8). Never "fix" a pastel by nudging L/a/b; replace it with the chord color.

4. EXTENSION TO NEW PIGMENTS. A new pigment is lift-eligible iff (i) EXTENSION RULE §0: hue ∈ [9°,89°] or 155–165°, ≥90° CIELCH from every existing foreground pigment at intended use, exactly one role; (ii) core C_P ≳ 45 — below that G1+G2 are jointly infeasible (sienna #A66838, h61 C42: α*=0.140 → C28.8 < 29, REJECT on G2; it stays umber-family, text-only); (iii) compute α* by the generator and verify all four gates. The warm band is too densely packed for any new hue to clear ≥90° separation, so new pigments are new members of existing families (second gold, second ochre) or the one complement slot.


### Glow/shadow polarity — the chiaroscuro grammar

# GLOW/SHADOW POLARITY — chiaroscuro grammar (executable)

**0. Material derivation (metal vs dye).** Glow is a MATERIAL property, not brightness. Gold is the METAL — core #C9962E (h81, L65, C59), pale #E5C193 (h77, L80, C29) — the manuscript's light source. Madder (h39), saffron (h75), verdigris (h159) are DYES: they absorb, they never emit. LUMINOSITY (brightness) is open to any pigment at its 300-tint (coral L66, sage L69, saffron L81); GLOW (self-illumination: bloom, halo token, blur feather ≥4px, box-shadow spread >0) is gold's alone. Emitter test: any glow token whose hue is outside the gold family band h∈[70°,90°] is an illegal emitter.

**1. Emitter vs mark — same metal, two readings.** On dark, gold is an EMITTER: pale gold #E5C193 reads brighter than the lamp-darkened parchment because gold leaf reflects; glow = halo #F0DFBD @0.85, feather ≤2× core radius (hard cap). On light, gold is a MARK: 500-core #C9962E (L65) is drawn amber line against bright parchment (2.5:1 — a line, not light); no bloom on parchment. The two golds are one chord sampled emitter-ward (C29) and pigment-ward (C59).

**2. Single-glow rule + failure modes.** Exactly ONE glow token per viewport = one lamp per manuscript. A second glow fails three provable ways: (a) MATERIAL VIOLATION — a dye glow (saffron h75 halo, verdi h159 halo) collapses metal/dye into a dashboard; rejection = glow hue ∉ [70°,90°]. (b) POLARITY FLIP — a second, cool emitter inverts the warm-side reading of the indigo shadow, so the shadow direction is undefined (two sources, two cast sides) and the shadow reads as mud. (c) DOUBLE-DOT — two gold glows within 2× halo radius merge; merge into one, never stack.

**3. Indigo = the cool shadow; warm-light/cool-shadow mechanics.** Light ground is warm light (parchment h89). Aerial perspective: warm advances, cool recedes — so shadow hue must lie in the wheel's cordoned shadow-only band h∈[200°,300°], i.e. ≥120° from the h77–89 light. Indigo #181B2D (h289, L10, C13) is the ONLY legal shadow hue; achromatic black is forbidden because black-dilution crosses the gray core (C→0) and reads green-mud on warm parchment. Indigo's role is forced by the light's hue — it is shadow, not "support."

**4. Shadow-tint + source-count rules.** SHADOW(surface, L) = sRGB_lerp(Indigo 500 #181B2D, surface, p), with p ≤ 0.60 so the indigo share never drops below 40% (guardrail keeping h≥200°). Light theme: L2 raised card → p=0.60, opacity 0.14, blur 8px, offset 0/2; L3 floating/modal → p=0.45, opacity 0.20, blur 24px, offset 0/4. Shadow sources ≤1 per scene (mirrors the single glow; one lamp ⇒ one cast). L1 hairlines carry NO shadow — use the umber 300 rule instead.

**5. Depth-mechanism complementarity (dark drops indigo).** depth(theme) = complement(ground). Light ground = warm light → depth = cool shadow (indigo). Dark ground = near-black (wall L2, panel L6, no deeper), so indigo (L10/C13) cannot separate from the umber/ink chrome stack and a dark indigo shadow is invisible noise. Dark depth = warm LIGHT drawn, never cast: pale-gold frame #E5C193 (10.9:1 on panel, 11.9:1 on deep-umber wall), pale-gold hairlines, panel lift to #1B1114. Rules: shadow sources = 0 in dark; indigo tokens are deleted; the frame, not the canvas edge, carries figure-ground.
